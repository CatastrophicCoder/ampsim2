Profile (macOS `sample`, 30 s of the DI bench; share of samples):

| Part | Share |
|---|---|
| Dense linear solves in Newton (`solveLinear<10>` B56, `<8>` preamp) | 32 % |
| Device equations (exp, pow, log1p, log, Wright omega, Koren evaluations) | ≈ 26 % |
| Newton bookkeeping (J = I + Z·∂i/∂v, residuals) | ≈ 19 % |
| Network matrix–vector products (`RootNetwork::process`) | 11 % |
| Half-step trigger's Thevenin voltages (`thevenin`, the same product computed again) | 4.4 % |
| Element waves, memsets, rest | ≈ 7 % |

Per block (M4 bench, idle): Preamp 1263–1857 ns, PowerAmp 1699–2286 ns per engine sample. Reaching a
real-time factor of 10 at 16x needs ≈ 24× less work per sample. Candidates (none applied, SPEC M5: report
first): reuse of the Thevenin product (4 %); the device Jacobians are block-diagonal, so J = I + Z·D and its
solve can exploit the structure (the 10×10 solve dominates); chord Newton (reuse the factorisation over
iterations / samples); lookup tables for the device equations (WDF_DESIGN §4 M6 list); linear parts at a lower
rate; a lower internal rate (the table above shows its accuracy cost without SPICE verification).

## Newton under automation (Q21)

All first-solve failures are in the power amp (B56; the preamp never needs more than 7 iterations); they
occur at high Master, also with static knobs: 5 s of DI with every knob at 1.0: 865 (Master 1.0 alone: 89;
Master + Presence 1.0: 169), all rescued. Experiment (reverted): a B56 iteration limit of 16 / 20 instead
of 12 gives 10 / 7 first-solve failures on that case at the same cost; half-step triggers 50 / 20 V with 12
iterations give 316 / 310. The Newton checks are written against the configured limit 12 (WDF_DESIGN §4).
